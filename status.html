How the pace was reached

Three changes, and they compound.

  • A production systemEvery site follows one documented route: corroborate the client's own published record, write down every fact with its source, choose a design that suits that person's working life, build, audit, publish, verify. The route is written down, so it does not live in anyone's head and it does not drift between sites.
  • Parallel productionSeveral builds run at once without colliding. Each reserves its client, its design and its working files before starting, so two builds can never land on the same person. Reserving is atomic: one wins, the other is told immediately and moves on. Nothing is duplicated and nothing is quietly overwritten.
  • No design used twiceEvery design is checked against a ledger before a build begins. Two clients will never receive the same page with a different accent colour. This answers the point raised in the last report about three sites reading as one page reproduced three times.

Throughput is roughly two and a half to three and a half hours per complete site, about half of it the bespoke photography set.

Quality, measured rather than asserted

Every site is proved before it is published, not eyeballed.

Contrast is measured pixel by pixel

Text sitting over a photograph cannot be checked by reading a stylesheet. Every piece of text on the page is rendered, its exact letter shapes located, the colour underneath each one sampled, and the real contrast ratio calculated against the WCAG AA floors. Hidden menus are opened and measured too, so nothing escapes the count.

Every page is rendered before release

Zero console errors and zero failed requests, or it does not ship.

Every file is verified after publishing

A page that loads is not proof the upload was complete. Each published file is fetched back and compared against the original byte for byte, which catches a silently incomplete upload before a client ever sees it.

Imagery

Each client receives twelve bespoke photorealistic images built around their own working life, generated from a reference photograph with their consent.

The amber light of a lithography bay for a diagnostics founder. Mud on a gauge wheel for a farmer and inventor. A library aisle for a theologian.

The images come from what the person actually does rather than a generic shot list. No two images in a set repeat a location or an outfit, and at least eight of the twelve are specific enough that they could only be that client.

Three rules protect the client. No readable text is generated anywhere in an image, because invented text renders imperfectly and is the most obvious tell on a professional's site. No awards, certificates, plaques or medals are ever generated, because a generated credential is a fabricated credential. And every site states plainly, in the client's own gallery, that the pictures are AI-generated illustrations produced with their approval from a reference photograph.

Accuracy

Every statistic, date, quotation, affiliation and award on a client site comes from that client's own published material. Nothing is inferred and nothing is filled in. Where a detail cannot be confirmed against a primary source, it is left off the page and recorded as needing the client's confirmation.

Each site carries a written record of every claim and its source, kept alongside the build. If a client ever questions a line, the answer is on file with the date and the citation.

Hosting

Twenty-eight sites run on GitHub Pages under a single account, which is free and carries no deployment limit. Three earlier sites remain on Netlify and continue to serve normally.

Closed since the last report

Three things the last report listed as outstanding are now finished.

  • Four contact forms correctedShoemaker, Chvatal, Berry and Grimes each had an enquiry form posting to my own address rather than the client’s, so every enquiry was reaching the wrong inbox. All four now post to the client. Each corrected file was compared against the live version first to prove only the form address changed, then fetched back after publishing and compared byte for byte before being called done.

Marquis and its competitors distribute across several, and three of those eight clients had full placements on channels the sweep never looked at. Two are now built and live; one more is ready to build. The search now runs across every channel and on the open web, not one wire.
